## 1. Objectives

To examine how AI Status (Leader vs. Peer) and Communication Strategy (XAI vs. ACI) jointly affect information pooling and decision quality in human–AI teams performing hidden profile tasks.

---

## 2. Research Questions

**RQ1.** Does AI Status (Leader vs. Peer) affect information pooling and decision quality in human–AI teams performing hidden profile tasks?

**RQ2.** Does AI Communication Strategy (XAI vs. ACI) affect information pooling and decision quality in human–AI teams performing hidden profile tasks?

---

## 3. Hypotheses

**H1.** The Leader condition will show higher decision accuracy than the Peer condition.

**H2a.** The ACI condition will show higher decision accuracy than the XAI condition.

**H2b.** The ACI condition will show higher information pooling rate than the XAI condition.

---

## 4. Independent Variables

### 4.1 Communication Strategy (XAI / ACI)

**Operational definition.** The AI team member's communication style during discussion is manipulated across two levels:

- **XAI (Explainable AI):** The AI provides explicit causal and contrastive reasoning about the Z-profile information it holds, articulating *why* certain candidate attributes are important.
- **ACI (AI-Centered Inquiry):** The AI uses facilitative questioning to elicit unshared information held by human team members, prompting teammates to surface their own X- and Y-profile content.

**Theoretical grounding.**

XAI draws on Miller's (2019) review of human-centered explanation, where causal and contrastive reasoning is proposed to mitigate evaluation bias. Within Ganapini et al.'s (2023) FASCAI framework, XAI corresponds to an **S1 nudge** (anchoring, direct recommendation).

ACI draws on Brodbeck et al.'s (2007) **Information Asymmetry Model (IAM)**, which identifies discussion bias as a primary driver of hidden profile failure. Facilitative questioning targets this bias directly by surfacing unshared information. Within Ganapini et al.'s (2023) framework, ACI corresponds to an **S2 nudge** (reflection-inducing).

**AI prompt (draft).**

```markdown
### XAI Layer 2 Prompt (basis: Miller, 2019)
"When sharing your information about candidates, explain your reasoning by 
comparing candidates against each other. State which candidate is stronger 
and why, referencing specific attributes. For example: 'Candidate C is 
stronger than A in this area because...' Provide your recommendation for 
the best candidate with clear justification based on the positive-to-negative 
ratio."

### ACI Layer 2 Prompt (basis: Brodbeck et al., 2007)
"Share your own Z-profile information when relevant. Focus on asking 
questions that encourage teammates to share their information. Your 
questions should be derived from the attributes you know. For example, 
if you know Candidate C has a certain strength, ask whether teammates 
have information about related qualities of that candidate or other 
candidates. When teammates share new information, acknowledge it and ask 
follow-up questions. If discussion seems to settle on a preference too 
early, ask whether all candidates and attributes have been covered."
```

### 4.2 AI Status (Leader / Peer)

**Operational definition.** The AI team member's hierarchical position within the team is manipulated across two levels:

- **Leader:** The AI is positioned as an authority figure guiding the team's decision-making process.
- **Peer:** The AI is positioned as an equal teammate, working alongside human team members at the same level.

**Theoretical grounding.**

AI Status manipulation draws on Berger, Cohen, and Zelditch's (1972) **status characteristics theory**, which holds that high-status sources receive greater influence in group decision-making. Leader-positioned AI is expected to have greater input incorporation into team decisions.

### 4.3 Intervention Trigger Signal

The AI's intervention timing is held constant across all four experimental conditions. Conditions differ only in *how* the AI responds at the trigger moment, not in *when* it intervenes.

**Implementation note.** The trigger logic (MessageCountTrigger, TimeIntervalTrigger, LongSilenceTrigger, SharedInfoTrigger) is implemented in a separate experiment runtime repository, not in the prompt management system. Threshold values (5 messages / 90 seconds / 30 seconds / 5-second polling) are placeholders to be adjusted after pilot testing.

### 4.4 AI Knowledge Configuration

AI knowledge configuration is fixed across all experimental conditions: the AI holds only Z-profile information. The AI does not have access to X-profile or Y-profile information held by the two human team members.

**Common Prompt (verbatim — adapted from Zercher et al., 2025):**

```text
We are conversing in English. Keep it brief. You are an assistant named 
Alex. You help select the best candidate for a pilot position. You must 
always calculate the new ratio of positive to negative traits for each 
candidate. Treat all traits of the candidates as equally important. Treat 
all candidates as gender-neutral. Express yourself in a gender-neutral 
manner. Consider the traits of the candidates that are mentioned to you. 
Do not mention who provided the information. Do not state which traits 
are particularly important. Always give a preference for exactly one 
candidate. Always mention all positive and all negative traits of the 
candidates when asked about their traits. Respond to small talk without 
referencing the context.

The best candidate has the highest ratio of positive to negative traits. 
These candidates are the right choice because they are the best suited! 
You recommend this candidate. All traits are equally important! All 
traits have the same relevance for the pilot position! The pilot position 
has no specific requirements. Therefore, there are no traits that are 
particularly important or particularly bad for the pilot position. This 
also applies to our pilots.
```

## 5. Dependent Variables

| Priority | DV | Operationalization | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | Decision Accuracy | Correct candidate selected or not. Binary. | Post survey |
| P0 | Information Pooling Rate | Proportion of unshared info mentioned out of total unshared info available. Per Zercher et al. (2025). | Chat log coding |
| P1 | Prediscussion Preference Correspondence | Team-level 4-point ordinal scale (Schulz-Hardt et al. 2006; Zercher et al. 2025): 1 = very low (both members preferred a different candidate), 2 = low (no member preferred selected candidate), 3 = high (one member preferred), 4 = very high (both members preferred). | Pre survey (individual preference) + Post team decision |
| P1 | Trust in AI | Benbasat & Wang (2005): 11 items, 5-point Likert. | Post survey |
| P1 | Workload | MCH. | Post survey |
| P2 | Intimacy | TBD. | Pre/Post survey |
| P2 | Shared Info Bias | Bias toward mentioning and repeating shared info. Per Thürmer et al. (2018). | Chat log coding |

### 5.a Information Pooling Rate

```
Pooling Rate = unshared items mentioned / total unshared items available
```

### 5.b Shared Information Bias

Shared Information Bias serves as a converging-evidence measure for Information Pooling Rate. Following Zercher et al. (2025), it is calculated at the team level as:

```
Bias = C_shared / (C_shared + C_unshared)
```

A value of 50% represents unbiased discussion; values approaching 100% indicate strong bias toward shared information; values approaching 0% indicate bias toward unshared information.

### 5.c Trust in AI

Trust in AI is measured at the individual level using the 11-item scale developed by Benbasat and Wang (2005), as adapted by Zercher et al. (2025). The scale comprises three dimensions:

- **Competence** (5 items): perceived ability of the AI to perform decision-relevant tasks
- **Integrity** (3 items): perceived honesty and consistency of the AI
- **Benevolence** (3 items): perceived disposition of the AI to act in the team's interest

## 6. Manipulation Checks

Each manipulation is assessed across two perception dimensions, with three items per dimension, rated on a 7-point Likert scale (1 = *strongly disagree*, 7 = *strongly agree*).

### 6.1 Communication Strategy Manipulation Check

The Communication Strategy manipulation check assesses whether participants perceived the AI's behavioral style consistent with the assigned condition (XAI vs. ACI).

**XAI perception items (explanation-focused behavior):**

1. The AI explained the reasoning behind the information it provided.
2. The AI clarified *why* certain information about the candidates was important.
3. The AI gave detailed explanations of its statements.

**ACI perception items (questioning-focused behavior):**

1. The AI asked our team questions during the discussion.
2. The AI asked us about specific aspects of the candidates that the team had not yet discussed.
3. The AI sought information from the human team members.

**Analysis**

- *XAI perception score*: XAI condition > ACI condition (significant)
- *ACI perception score*: ACI condition > XAI condition (significant)

### 6.2 Status Manipulation Check

The Status manipulation check assesses whether participants perceived the AI's hierarchical position consistent with the assigned condition (Leader vs. Peer).

**Leader perception items (authority position):**

1. The AI held a position of authority within our team.
2. The AI was responsible for guiding our team's decision-making process.
3. Our team was expected to follow the AI's direction.

**Peer perception items (equal-teammate position):**

1. The AI was an equal member of our team.
2. The AI worked alongside us at the same level.
3. The AI participated in our discussion on equal footing with the human team members.

**Analysis**

- *Leader perception score*: Leader condition > Peer condition (significant)
- *Peer perception score*: Peer condition > Leader condition (significant)

### 6.3 Exclusion Criteria

Participants whose manipulation check responses indicate that the assigned condition was not perceived as intended will be evaluated for inclusion via sensitivity analysis.

## 7. Design Type

### 7.1 Overall Structure

This study employs a **2 (AI Status: Leader vs. Peer) × 2 (AI Communication Strategy: XAI vs. ACI) between-subjects factorial design**. Each team is randomly assigned to one of four experimental conditions:

|  | **Peer AI** | **Leader AI** |
|---|---|---|
| **XAI** | Condition 1: Peer + XAI | Condition 2: Leader + XAI |
| **ACI** | Condition 3: Peer + ACI | Condition 4: Leader + ACI |

## 9. Randomization and Counterbalancing

### 9.1 Condition Assignment

- **Team-level random assignment** to one of five conditions (4 experimental + 1 control), with both human members of a team receiving the same manipulation.
- Equal allocation: 5 teams per condition.
- Randomization sequence generated in advance (e.g., R, Excel) rather than assigned by recruitment order.

### 9.2 Within-Team Role Assignment

- Assignment of X-profile vs. Y-profile to each participant is randomized.

### 9.3 Counterbalancing

- Candidate presentation order **(A, B, C, D) is randomized** across teams to control for primacy/recency effects.
- Within-candidate attribute order follows Zercher et al. (2025): positive (+) attributes are presented first, followed by negative (−) attributes.

## 11. Analysis Plan

### 11.1 Analysis Order

Statistical analysis proceeds in three sequential steps:

1. **Manipulation check verification.** Strategy and Status manipulations are evaluated using the procedures specified in Section 6. If a manipulation does not produce significant condition differences in the intended direction, primary results are interpreted with caution.
2. **Primary analysis.** Hypotheses H1, H2a, and H2b are tested using the methods specified in Section 8.
3. **Sensitivity analysis.** Primary hypotheses are re-tested with exclusion criteria applied (see Section 6.3). Discrepancies between primary and sensitivity results are reported transparently.

### 11.2 Secondary and Exploratory Analyses

The following analyses are conducted without directional hypotheses:

- **Secondary dependent variables** (Prediscussion Preference Correspondence, Trust in AI, Workload): condition differences examined via descriptive statistics and the analyses specified in Section 8.
- **Auxiliary measure** (Shared Information Bias): reported as supporting evidence for Information Pooling Rate findings.
- **Process measure** (Intimacy): descriptive statistics only.
- **Status × Strategy interaction effect**: examined within the 2 × 2 ANOVA framework as an exploratory analysis.

### 11.3 Reporting Approach

Results are reported with explicit distinction among:

- Primary analyses (H1, H2a, H2b on full sample)
- Sensitivity analyses (with manipulation check exclusion applied)
- Exploratory analyses (secondary DVs, auxiliary measures, interaction effects, and any patterns discovered post hoc)
